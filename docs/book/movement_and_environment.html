Satya TTRPG Movement, Encumbrance, Vehicles & Environmental Survival

Rust Engine Implementation: crates/satya-engine/src/environment_vehicles.rs (MovementGait, EncumbranceTier, VehicleClass, VehicleProfile, evaluate_movement_velocities, evaluate_special_locomotion, evaluate_encumbrance, evaluate_vehicle_triad_exchange, evaluate_collision_and_gloc, canonical_vehicle_catalog) and crates/satya-engine/src/environmental_hazards.rs (DehydrationStage, StarvationPhase, HypothermiaStage, HeatIllnessStage, HypoxiaZone, ToxinClass, evaluate_dehydration, evaluate_starvation, evaluate_thermal_stress, evaluate_sleep_deprivation, evaluate_altitude_hypoxia, evaluate_toxin_or_pathogen, evaluate_radiation_exposure).

This chapter defines the canonical Movement, Load-Bearing Encumbrance, Vehicular Pursuits & G-Force Physics, and Environmental Survival Systems for Satya (for definitions of all mathematical variables and formulas such as SF, Vsprint, Wreq, CLO, Sv, see the Mathematical Notation, Variables & Formula Glossary). In keeping with Satya's simulation philosophy, movement speed is not an arbitrary fixed integer ("30 feet per turn") shared by every human regardless of stature, conditioning, or backpack weight, nor are environmental hazards hand-waved when operators deploy into deserts, mountains, maritime storms, or depressurized orbital habitats.

1. Movement Architecture: Combat & Non-Combat Gaits

A character's movement velocity is governed by their Speed (SPD), Fitness (FIT), Kinesthesis (KIN), Height (HGT stride length), Encumbrance Tier, and any Lower-Extremity Wounds (which directly slash their Tempo Cap Tmax).

1.1 Baseline Velocity Formulas (SPD, FIT & HGT)

  1. Stride Factor (SF):
    SF = HGT1.75 m
    (Equals 1.00 for a 1.75 m human; ~1.09 for a 1.90 m tall runner; ~0.91 for a 1.60 m character).
  2. Maximum Sprint Velocity (Vsprint in Meters per Second):
    Vsprint = (4.0 + 0.35 × SPDeff) × SF
    • At Human Average (SPD 10, HGT 1.75 m): 7.50 m/s (27.0 km/h, or 1.88 meters per Tick since 1 second = 4 Ticks).
    • At Athletic (SPD 14): 8.90 m/s (32.0 km/h, or 2.23 m/Tick).
    • At Peak Human (SPD 18, Olympic Sprinter): 10.30 m/s (37.1 km/h, or 2.58 m/Tick — matching a sub-10-second 100m dash!).
  3. Sustained Overland Pace Factor: While SPD governs explosive short-term burst speed, Fitness (FIT) governs how long a character can sustain a run or forced march without accumulating Fatigue (fatigue_level).

1.2 Tactical Combat Movement (Meters per Tick & Triad Interaction)

1.3 Special Movement Modes (Jumping, Climbing, Swimming & Zero-G)

  1. Jumping & Vaulting (Jumping / Parkour KIN/SPD, powered by POW):
    • Running Broad Jump: Base distance = 2.0 m + (0.25 m × POW) + (0.3 m × Cascade Successes) (4.5 m baseline at POW 10).
    • Standing Broad Jump: 55% of Running Broad Jump (~2.5 m at POW 10).
    • Running High Jump / Wall Scaling: Base vertical clearance = 0.6 m + (0.06 m × POW) + (0.1 m × Successes) (1.2 m waist/chest vault without hands; add 0.8 × HGT when grabbing a ledge with hands and pulling up via FOR/KIN).
  2. Climbing (FOR/KIN):
    • Ladder / Knotted Rope: 0.5 m/Tick (2.0 m/s).
    • Rough Wall / Drainpipe / Rock Face: 0.15 m/Tick (0.6 m/s), requiring a Climbing (FOR/KIN) cascade check with Δext set by surface holds and Encumbrance Tier (+2 per Encumbrance Tier).
  3. Swimming (Swimming & Diving FIT/KIN):
    • Unencumbered surface swim speed is 20% of Vsprint (1.5 m/s or 0.38 m/Tick at SPD 10, FIT 10).
    • Encumbrance in Water: Tier 2 (Tactical Load) imposes +4 Δext to stay afloat; Tier 3+ (Heavy Load / steel plates without flotation) drags the character downward at 1.0 m/s unless jettisoned (12 ticks quick-release cutout)!
  4. Zero-G / Microgravity EVA Movement (Zero-G Maneuver KIN/INS):
    • In orbital or deep-space microgravity, movement follows Newtonian momentum: pushing off a bulkhead imparts a constant velocity vector (v = Impulse / MAS) that continues indefinitely until arrested by a handhold, mag-boot lock, or RCS thruster burst.
    • Missing a Zero-G Maneuver check while translating across an open bay leaves the character Adrift (Tempo = 0 for directional changes unless firing a thruster or tether).

2. Encumbrance & Load-Bearing Architecture

Every piece of armor, weapon, ammunition magazine, battery, ration, and medical kit has physical mass in kilograms (kg). Satya models encumbrance continuously from the character's Force (FOR), Body Mass (MAS), and Load-Bearing Ergonomics.

2.1 Calculating Base Comfort Load (Lbase)

A character's Base Uninhibited Load (Lbase)—the weight they can wear or carry in balanced webbing without any degradation to combat speed or reflexes—is:

Lbase = (MAS70.0 kg) × (FOR × 1.2 kg)
  • Human Average (MAS = 70 kg, FOR = 10): Lbase = 12.0 kg (~26.5 lbs — e.g., clothing, light covert vest, service pistol, spare mags, comms, and EDC kit).
  • Strong Operator (MAS = 85 kg, FOR = 14): Lbase = 20.4 kg (~45 lbs — can carry a carbine, plate carrier, and assault pack at Tier 0/1!).
  • Slight Civilian (MAS = 55 kg, FOR = 8): Lbase = 7.5 kg (~16.5 lbs).

2.2 Ergonomic Load Multipliers (How Weight Is Carried)

Not all kilograms are equal. Twenty kilograms distributed across a padded internal-frame rucksack and hip belt is vastly easier to move with than a 20 kg steel ammo crate carried in one's hands:

3. Vehicles, High-Speed Chases & Crash / G-Force Physics

Satya's Tactical Triad (Tempo, Control, Force) scales seamlessly from close-quarters foot combat to vehicular pursuits, aerial dogfights, and orbital docking:

  1. The Vehicle as a Physical Envelope:
    • A vehicle provides its own mechanical Envelope Ceilings: Top Speed / Acceleration (Vehicle Tempo Cap), Handling & Braking (Vehicle Control Cap), and Chassis Mass & Armor (Vehicle Force / Root Cap).
    • The driver/pilot fills this envelope using their Driving / Piloting Skill (N) and SPD / KIN (or DEX / FOC for remote drones), bounded by the vehicle's ceilings!
  2. Tactical Triad in a Chase or Dogfight:
    • Tempo (T): Throttle commitment—closing or opening the Chase Range Band (Bumper-to-Bumper ↔ Close [15m] ↔ Medium [50m] ↔ Long [150m+] ↔ Lost Visual).
    • Control (C): Steering precision—threading through oncoming traffic, drifting a hairpin corner without spinning out, holding a steady platform for passengers firing from windows, and evading PIT maneuvers.
    • Force (F): Ramming, PIT maneuvers, sideswiping, and holding the road (Vehicle Root) against collisions or crosswinds.
  3. Collision Kinetic Trauma & High-G Maneuvers:
    • Collisions: Impact severity scales with relative velocity (Δ v) and mass ratio (m1 / m2), inflicting Blunt Trauma across multiple random Hit Locations (mitigated by seatbelts, airbags, or crash harnesses).
    • High-G Blackout (G-LOC): In high-performance aircraft or orbital burn maneuvers exceeding 5G+, crew members must roll HLT / FIT (assisted by anti-G flight suits) or suffer Tunnel Vision (Dazzled) followed by G-LOC (Unconscious).

4.1 Hydration & Water Deprivation

Water is the most acute survival resource after breathable air. A human can survive weeks without food, but in arid heat or heavy combat exertion, lethal dehydration occurs within 24 to 72 hours.

Daily Water Requirement (Wreq)

  • Temperate Climate, Low Activity: 3.0 Liters / Day (3.0 kg mass).
  • Heavy Physical Labor / Forced March: 5.0 Liters / Day.
  • Hot / Arid Desert or Tropical Jungle (> 35°C) + Tactical Gear: 8.0 to 10.0 Liters / Day (0.5–0.8 L/hour sweat loss during daytime movement!).

The Five Stages of Dehydration

For every 25% of Daily Water Requirement missed (or every 2 Liters of unreplaced sweat deficit), the character advances +1 Dehydration Stage:

4.2 Nutrition, Caloric Deficit & Starvation

  • Daily Caloric Requirement:
    • Sedentary / Watch Duty: 2,000–2,400 kcal/day (~0.5 kg dry field rations).
    • Standard Patrol / Active Operations: 3,200–3,800 kcal/day (~0.75 kg MRE / nutrient ration).
    • Arctic Cold or Heavy Mountain March: 4,500–6,000 kcal/day (~1.2 kg high-fat rations).
  • Short-Term Fasting & Glycogen Depletion (Days 1–3 Without Food):
    • First 24 Hours: Mild hunger pangs (+0 mechanical penalty for characters with DIS >= 10; +1 Δint if DIS < 10).
    • 48–72 Hours (Glycogen Depletion & Early Ketosis): Liver glycogen empties; blood glucose dips. Imposes +2 to Δint, +1 fatigue_level floor, and -2 to FOC and FIT.
  • Prolonged Caloric Deficit & Starvation (Days 4–30+):
    • For every 3 Days of total starvation (or every cumulative 10,000 kcal deficit), the character must make a HLT check (Δext = +2 × Week):
    • Catabolic Muscle & Mass Loss: Body Mass (MAS) drops by -1.5 kg, and Force (FOR), Fitness (FIT), and Health (HLT) each erode by -1 point (+2 Δint per week).
    • When HLT or FOR reaches 0 (typically after 30–45 days without food depending on starting MAS and HLT), fatal organ failure occurs.

4.3 Temperature Extremes: Cold (Hypothermia) & Heat (Hyperthermia)

Human core body temperature must remain within a narrow window around 37.0°C (98.6°F). Both extreme cold and extreme heat are governed by the balance between Ambient Effective Temperature (including windchill or humidity), Clothing Insulation / Cooling (CLO Rating), and Metabolic Heat Production.

A. Cold Exposure, Hypothermia & Frostbite

When exposed to temperatures below their clothing's comfort threshold (accelerated ×10 if submerged in cold water or soaked in rain with high wind), the character rolls HLT / FIT every Hour (or every 5 Minutes in freezing water): 1. Cold Stress & Shivering (Core 36.0–35.0°C): Peripheral vasoconstriction numbs fingers and toes. Dexterity (DEX) and Tactile (TAC) reduced by -3; +2 Δint; +1 fatigue_level from constant shivering. 2. Mild-to-Moderate Hypothermia (Core 34.9–32.0°C): Violent shivering gives way to muscle rigidity and apathy. DEX, TAC, SPD, KIN, ANA, and FOC reduced by -6; +0.30 action_time_factor; +6 Δint. 3. Severe Hypothermia (Core < 32.0°C): Shivering stops; paradoxical undressing/delirium, followed by Unconsciousness and ventricular fibrillation. 4. Frostbite (Localized Tissue Freezing): In sub-zero windchill (< -10°C), exposed skin (fingers, nose, ears, toes) requires a separate HLT check or suffers Tier 1 (Frostnip: -2 DEX/TAC) to Tier 3/4 (Deep Frostbite: gangrene / permanent digit amputation) structural damage.

B. Heat Exposure, Heat Exhaustion & Heatstroke

When operating in high ambient temperatures (> 32°C dry or > 26°C at high humidity), especially while wearing body armor or performing high-exertion combat/marching:

  • Sweat Multiplier: Water consumption requirement increases by ×2 to ×4 (0.5–1.0 L/hour).
  • Every Hour (or every 15 Minutes of heavy combat/marching in armor), the character rolls FIT / HLT: 1. Heat Cramps (Stage 1): Painful spasms in calves and abdomen; -2 to Tmax and Fmax, +1 fatigue_level. 2. Heat Exhaustion (Stage 2 — Core 38.5–39.9°C): Profuse sweating, pallor, throbbing headache, Dizzy (-4 KIN) and Nausea (+4 Δint); +3 fatigue_level. Must stop exertion, remove armor, and cool/hydrate in shade immediately. 3. Heatstroke (Stage 3 — Core >= 40.0°C / 104°F — Medical Emergency): Thermoregulation fails completely; skin becomes hot and dry, CNS dysfunction triggers delirium, seizures, and Coma. Inflicts +1 Tier of systemic organ trauma every 15 minutes until core temperature is rapidly cooled (ice bath, evaporative cooling).

4.4 Sleep Deprivation, Atmospheric Pressure, Radiation & Toxins

  1. Sleep Deprivation & Circadian Debt:
    • Humans require 6–8 hours of sleep per 24-hour cycle (4 hours minimum maintenance with a successful DIS check).
    • 24 Hours Awake: +2 Δint, -1 to FOC, INS, ITU, and OODA.
    • 48 Hours Awake: +4 Δint, -3 to FOC, ANA, INS, ITU, MEM, OODA, +2 fatigue_level floor.
    • 72+ Hours Awake: +8 Δint, -6 to all Mental & Sensory Characteristics; auditory/visual micro-hallucinations; whenever stationary for more than 5 minutes, must pass a DIS check (Δext = +6) or fall into an involuntary Microsleep (Unconscious).
  2. Altitude & Atmospheric Hypoxia:
    • Above 2,500 meters (8,200 ft) unacclimatized (or in a partially depressurized habitat with low pO2): Fitness (FIT) is reduced by -2 per 1,000 meters above 2,000 m.
    • Above 5,500 meters without supplemental oxygen: Acute Mountain Sickness (Dizzy, Nausea), progressing to HAPE / HACE (High-Altitude Pulmonary / Cerebral Edema — HLT checks every 6 hours).
    • Hard Vacuum / Explosive Decompression (0 atm): Ebullism and rapid deoxygenation of venous blood in the lungs reduces useful consciousness to 12–16 seconds (48–64 Ticks), with -2 HLT cold/barotrauma per 20 ticks after unconsciousness.
  3. Toxins, Venoms, Chemical Agents & Pathogens (HLT Saves):
    • Model every poison, nerve agent, or biological pathogen with Four Parameters: Vector (Inhalation, Contact, Ingestion, Injection/Wound), Onset Delay (Ticks, Minutes, or Days), Virulence (Δext on the victim's HLT cascade save), and Target Effect (e.g., Tear Gas / CS: Onset 4 ticks, Virulence +2, Effect: Blinded/Dazzled + Coughing; Neurotoxin / Sarin: Onset 12 ticks, Virulence +10, Effect: Paralysis + Asphyxiation + Convulsions).
  4. Ionizing Radiation (Sievert / Gray Exposure Tiers):
    • Tracked in cumulative Acute Dose Equivalents (Sv):
    • < 0.5 Sv: Subclinical (no acute symptoms).
    • 0.5 – 1.5 Sv (Mild ARS): Nausea and +1 fatigue_level after 6–12 hours; temporary -2 HLT.
    • 1.5 – 4.0 Sv (Moderate-Severe Hematopoietic ARS): Vomiting within 2 hours, hair loss, severe leukopenia (HLT halved against infection over 2–6 weeks; requires bone-marrow/stem-cell therapy).
    • > 6.0 Sv (Lethal GI / Neurovascular ARS): Acute shock, internal mucosal hemorrhage, fatal within days without advanced 2035 nanomedical regeneration.
